Reading for the literal

R1 is the cheapest objective on the paper: the answer is printed in the text. This lesson is about stopping at what is there instead of guessing past it.

About 15 minutes One skill Paper 1 Q1(a)–(d)

Why this, now

Question 1 tests five objectives at once, but the first one is the foundation of the rest:

R1 demonstrate understanding of explicit meanings

0500/01 Specimen mark scheme, Question 1 — the assessment objectives

Explicit means stated. Not hinted, not suggested — printed. And the mark scheme pays for it in the simplest possible way:

1(c) Give two of the main ways in which horses were used by humans in the past.

Award 1 mark for each idea, up to maximum of 2.

0500/01 Specimen mark scheme

Two ideas, two marks, both of them sitting in one paragraph. If you lose marks here, you are not failing to understand the text. You are failing to locate — and locating is the one thing in Paper 1 you can make automatic.

The knowledge

R1 questions come in three shapes. All three are answered by the same move: find the words, then say what they say.

The three shapes of a literal question
ShapeExample from the specimenWhat you do
Name a fact1(a) "What is the other name for an Eohippus?"One word or phrase. No sentence needed.
Give N stated reasons1(c) "Give two of the main ways…"Count to N, then stop.
Unpack a word1(b)(ii) "'only faintly resembled'"Split the phrase into its parts and say each plainly.

Every literal question tells you where to look — "Reread paragraph 2", "lines 4–5". That is not politeness. It is a boundary. The examiner is saying: the answer is in these lines, and the rest of the page is there to tempt you.

The trap this lesson exists to close

The paragraph next to the answer usually contains something that sounds like the answer but belongs to a different question. Read the question's own words twice — especially small ones like “in the past”, “first”, “two” — before you read the paragraph.

Worked example

Here is 1(c) against the real paragraph. Read it once, then look at what was credited.

These early prototype horses were followed by bigger, better models. By the time humans arrived on Earth around 5 million years ago, the horse was already firmly established. Horses clearly interested our ancestors, inspiring artists as early as the Stone Age. Before engines, horses provided transport, though modern-day roles favour leisure and sport. But there’s a big difference between observing animals for artistic inspiration and harnessing them, so where and when, did horses first go from wild to tame?

0500/01 Specimen insert, Text A, paragraph 2
“Two of the main ways horses were used in the past”
From the paragraphMarksWhy
“inspiring artists as early as the Stone Age”1Artistic inspiration — credited as “used as subjects for art”.
“Before engines, horses provided transport”1Transport — and “before engines” is what makes it past.
“modern-day roles favour leisure and sport”0Modern, not past. The question's word “past” rules it out.
“observing animals for artistic inspiration”0Observing is not a use. The second half is already used above.

The accepted answers, in the mark scheme's own words: artistic inspiration / inspired (Stone Age) cave painters / used as subjects for art, and transport. Two ideas. The paragraph contains four sentences that could plausibly be about horses and people; only two of them answer this question.

Where your answers go

The box is scratch space — it does not save or send anything. Write your answer, press Copy answer, then paste it into the chat with your teacher. That is where marking happens.

1. “Use your own words to explain what the text means by ‘only faintly resembled’ (line 5).” — 2 marks

Literal, but in two strands. One word in the phrase carries each strand. Do not explain what the horse evolved into — that is a different question.

0 words
Accepted answers — 1 mark per strand

Strand 1 — “faintly”: hardly · scarcely · barely · just weakly · just slightly · not much

Strand 2 — “resembled”: looked like · appeared similar to

So: “It looked only slightly like the horse we know today.” Two marks. If you wrote “it was a different species”, you have jumped to paragraph 5 — accurate, and worth nothing here.

2. “Identify two reasons why scientists researching horse domestication thought that Iberia might have been the first place where horses were tamed.” — 2 marks

The paragraph names three possible reasons; the question asks for two. Give two different ones — not one explained twice.

0 words
Accepted answers — any two, 1 mark each

• physical proof discovered that horses lived there a long time ago / scientists found ancient horse samples
• lots of pictures of horses — cave art of other domesticated animals alongside horses, suggesting horses may also have been tamed then
• detailed, life-sized pictures suggest they had the opportunity to get up close, not just observe from a distance

Note what is not credited: “it seemed promising”. That is the text's summary of the evidence, not a reason. The marks are in the samples and the paintings.

What this is worth

Question 1 is 20 marks, and every one of them is point-marked. Literal items alone are about half of it. They are the marks that should never be lost — and the ones most often lost to reading a neighbouring sentence.
