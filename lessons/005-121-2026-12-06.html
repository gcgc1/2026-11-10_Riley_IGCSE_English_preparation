Reading for the implied

Some answers are never printed. R2 asks you to infer them — from a word, not from a guess.

About 15 minutes One skill Paper 1 Q1(d), Q1(e)(ii), Q2(b)

Why this, now

Half of Question 1 is literal. The other half is this:

R2 demonstrate understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes

0500/01 Specimen mark scheme, Question 1 — the assessment objectives

And one item makes the demand unusually explicit — read the instruction, not just the question:

1(e)(ii) Explain why scientists now no longer believe that modern domestic horses originated from Botai.

Award 1 mark for each idea, predominantly in own words, up to a maximum of 3. Answers which are entirely in the words of the text should not be credited.

0500/01 Specimen mark scheme

Three marks, and the text will not give you any of them. Notice the difference from last week's items: 1(c) and 1(d) were literal — the answers were sitting in the paragraph. Here the answer has to be built from what the text means.

The knowledge

An implied meaning has two parts, and you need both:

What makes an inference an inference
PartWhat it looks likeWhat happens without it
The evidenceA word or phrase in the text you could point toYou are guessing, and the examiner cannot credit a guess
The conclusionWhat that word means, in your own wordsYou are lifting, and 1(e)(ii) gives that nothing
The test that takes two seconds

Cover the text and read your answer. If it still contains the text's own words, you have not inferred. If it could have been written without reading the passage, you have guessed. A real inference is neither.

Worked example

Here are the two paragraphs 1(e)(ii) points at. The question is not “what happened?” — it is “why did they stop believing?”

But the archaeological site that really captivated researchers was the ancient settlement at Botai. Here excavations produced an astonishing 300 000 bone fragments: over 90 per cent were derived from horses, with evidence that mouthpieces (used when riding horses) may have damaged their teeth. Scientists found pottery shards containing chemical traces of mare’s milk, potentially consumed as yogurt or cheese. In addition, fenced-off areas within the structure where groups of horses could have been kept meant that Botai was seen as the most likely place that horses were first tamed, though there was no way to confirm this or be sure these were the horses that spread around the world as human-managed livestock.

More recently, scientists analysed DNA from as far back as 42 800 years ago. The result: horses in Botai were not the genetic origin for modern domestic horses, but a distinct smaller species naturally prone to dental issues. Evidence from elsewhere indicates that horses may have been domesticated independently by different groups of people, showing the importance of horses.

0500/01 Specimen insert, Text A, paragraphs 4–5
The four credited ideas, and the words they come from
MarkThe textWhat it implies
1“there was no way to confirm this”No way to corroborate the theory — it could not be proved.
1“not the genetic origin for modern domestic horses”The DNA showed Botai horses were not the ancestors of today's horses.
1“naturally prone to dental issues”The damaged teeth had a different explanation — not mouthpieces.
1“Evidence from elsewhere indicates…”Other places show horses were tamed, so Botai is not the only origin.

Four ideas, and only three marks are available — the mark scheme offers more than it pays, so any three will do. Now look at the difference in how you write them:

Same idea, two versions
VersionMarksWhy
“Horses in Botai were not the genetic origin for modern domestic horses.”0Entirely the text's words. The instruction forbids crediting it.
“The DNA showed the Botai animals were a different, smaller breed, so today's horses cannot be descended from them.”1The evidence is named, the conclusion is in the candidate's own words.

Now do it

Two tasks. The first is a real question; the second takes an answer apart and rebuilds it.

Where your answers go

The box is scratch space — it does not save or send anything. Write your answer, press Copy answer, then paste it into the chat with your teacher. That is where marking happens.

1. “Explain what the writer suggests about horses in the phrase: ‘elegant, living machines, powered only by grass’.” — 3 marks

Three ideas are credited. Work word by word: what does “machines” imply, what does “powered only by grass” imply, and what does “elegant” add? Write what each one suggests — not what it says.

0 words
Accepted ideas — 1 mark each, any three

• beautiful creatures
• industrial strength levels
• incredible to think they are fuelled naturally
• environmentally sound form of transport / green form of energy — horsepower

A full-mark shape: “Calling a horse a machine suggests it worked with the strength of an engine, while ‘powered only by grass’ makes that strength seem impossible — no fuel, no factory, just a field.”

2. This is a lifted answer to 1(e)(ii): “Horses in Botai were not the genetic origin for modern domestic horses.” Rewrite it in your own words, then add one more reason the scientists changed their minds.

Two jobs: transform the sentence, then add a different idea. If your second reason is just the first one reworded, it earns nothing.

0 words
Model — 2 marks

“The DNA showed the Botai animals were a separate, smaller breed, so today's horses cannot have descended from them. Their worn teeth also have a natural explanation: that breed was prone to dental problems, so the damage is not proof that people rode them.”

Mark 1 for the transformed DNA point; mark 2 for the teeth. The second sentence does real work — it removes the evidence that made Botai look convincing in the first place.

What this is worth

R2 runs through the paper: Q1(d), Q1(e), all of Q2(b), and the attitude behind Q3(c) and Q4. Learning to separate “what it says” from “what it means” is the difference between a B and an A on Paper 1.
