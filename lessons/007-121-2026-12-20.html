Attitude: three details that prove it

Last week you named the attitude. This week you prove it — and the mark scheme is precise about what proof is worth.

About 15 minutes One skill Paper 2 Q1(a) · Paper 1 Q2(b)

Why this, now

The question tells you exactly how much evidence to give:

2(b) Assess the writer’s attitude towards the cloning of endangered animals. Use three details from Text B to support your answer.

0500/01 Specimen question paper

Three details. But look at what the band descriptor actually rewards — the word is not “three”:

Level 3 (4–5) Clear understanding of the writer’s attitude with relevant support from the text.

Level 2 (2–3) Some understanding of the writer’s attitude with some supporting detail.

0500/01 Specimen mark scheme, Table C (Reading)

So the three details are not three marks. There is no mark for a detail. There are five marks, and they are awarded for the quality of the link between the attitude and the evidence. Three details listed under a vague attitude score 2. One detail that genuinely proves a precisely named attitude can carry an answer into Level 3.

The knowledge

Support has a shape. Every credited detail does three things at once:

The three-part move
PartWhat it doesJaz example
ClaimNames the attitude precisely“She feels her effort is being stolen.”
DetailPoints at the exact words“profiting shamelessly from all my slaving away”
LinkSays what the detail proves“The adverb ‘shamelessly’ is her verdict: he gains without guilt, so she sees the contest as rigged.”

Beginners do Claim → Detail → Detail → Detail. The marks live in the third column.

Three ways to waste a detail

Repeating: three details that all show ambition are one idea in three coats. Summarising: a detail that retells the plot proves nothing about a feeling. Decorating: a long quotation with no link is a detail the examiner cannot use.

Worked example

Same extract as last week. This time, watch what happens when a detail is linked rather than listed.

‘I’d worked for months to cultivate one prospective client,’ explains Jaz. ‘Everyone knew winning a big order from that client could be a deciding factor in which of us from the international sales team got promoted. Then I discover from a junior colleague that Ravi’s also trying to net this wealthy individual, even though Ravi knew I was already in pursuit! I knew if I ignored the situation, he’d just seal the deal, profiting shamelessly from all my slaving away, and our boss would promote Ravi. But if I’d directly confronted Ravi, it could’ve made me look petty and selfish. At the time, I calculated the best way to fight back was to be just as devious as Ravi.’

0500/02 Specimen question paper, Question 1(a)
One attitude, three details, three links
DetailWhat it proves
“worked for months to cultivate”She invests time, so the client feels earned — which is why Ravi's shortcut feels like theft.
“profiting shamelessly”“Shamelessly” is a moral judgement: she is not just losing, she is being wronged.
“just as devious as Ravi”She knows the plan is underhand and chooses it anyway — resentment has turned into retaliation.

The three details are not interchangeable. Each one proves a different part of the same feeling: the effort, the injustice, the revenge. That is what “relevant support” means — relevance is a relationship, not a count.

A Level 3 answer — 5 marks

“Jaz is fiercely competitive and quietly resentful about her work. She treats a client as a prize she has earned: she had ‘worked for months to cultivate one prospective client’, so when Ravi chases the same person she calls it ‘profiting shamelessly from all my slaving away’. The adverb ‘shamelessly’ is her verdict on him — he gains without guilt — and it shows that what she fears is not only losing the sale but being treated as though her effort counted for nothing. By the end she decides the only fair response is to be ‘just as devious as Ravi’: she knows the plan is underhand and chooses it anyway, which turns resentment into retaliation.”

One attitude, three details, and every detail is followed by what it proves. Nothing is listed; everything is linked.

2. Look back at your three details. Choose the weakest — the one that proves the least — and replace it with a better one. Write the new sentence, and say in one line why the replacement is stronger.

The mark scheme's five attitudes are: competitive, ruthless/determined, ambitious/focused, hard-working/motivated, unfairly treated/anxious. Which of them is your current answer under-proving?

0 words
Model swap

Weak: “She is in the international sales team.” It proves nothing about a feeling.

Stronger: “She calls the client a ‘wealthy individual’ and the order ‘a deciding factor’ in the promotion, which shows that her competitiveness is not about the work itself but about being seen to win.”

Why it is stronger: it moves from a fact about her job to a fact about her priorities, so it can support “ambitious / focused”.

What this is worth

Paper 1 Q2(b) is 5 marks. Paper 2 Q1(a) is 5. Ten marks decided by whether your details are linked or merely listed — and the habit transfers to Q3(c), Q3(d) and Q4, where every quote has to earn its place.
